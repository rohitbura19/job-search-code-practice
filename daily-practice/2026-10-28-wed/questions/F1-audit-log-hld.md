# F1. Tamper-evident audit log service (HLD, 12 min out loud + 5 min self-review)

Feature of a big platform: every service must record who did what to which resource (login, role change, refund, data export). Compliance asks that the log is append-only, tamper-evident, searchable by actor / resource / time range, and kept 7 years. Design the audit log service other teams call.

Cover: API (`POST /events` single and batch with an idempotency key, `GET /events` with filters and keyset pagination, `GET /verify?from=&to=`), write path (async via Kafka for throughput vs sync for "must not lose" actions, outbox in the caller), the event schema and what is NEVER stored (secrets, full payloads), storage (append-only partitioned store: time-partitioned tables or object storage Parquet + a search index; why not update-in-place), the hash chain (per-partition chains so writers do not serialise, periodic anchoring of the head hash to an external WORM store / notarisation), access control (who can read what, audit of audit reads), retention and legal hold, multi-tenancy, and numbers: 20k events/s peak, 1.7B events/day * 400 B, p99 search < 2 s for a 7-day window, zero acknowledged-then-lost events.
Say out loud: what a hash chain proves and what it cannot (truncation of the tail, a malicious writer who owns the chain), and why you chain per shard and anchor externally.
