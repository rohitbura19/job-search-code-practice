## Principal-engineer closing round (10 min, speak out loud)

1. In E, two support agents press Save on the same case (both read version 3) while the pod that handled `POST /cases` for another case dies right after the commit and before the Kafka send. Walk through what each agent sees (status codes, ETags), what is in the database and in the topic, and what a downstream consumer never learns. Then: Kafka is down for 30 minutes. What does the API do today, what changes if you write an outbox row in the same transaction and let a relay publish it, what do you alert on (the age of the oldest unpublished row), and how does the consumer cope with the duplicates the relay will produce (an event id and an idempotent write, the 20 Oct consumer lesson)?
2. In F1, a driver arrives at a charger the app showed as FREE and it is occupied. Give four causes in order of likelihood (stale heartbeat, a flapping state, a lagging third-party feed, someone plugged in after the last event), what you measure for each, and what the app should have shown. Then: at 18:00 one status-stream partition lags by three minutes, and your 20 pods have slightly different views of the world. What does the user see, what is acceptable, and how do you make the staleness visible instead of hiding it?
3. In F2, the installer and the helper both edit "earth resistance" offline; the installer submits from his phone; the helper's phone syncs two hours later with a different value. What is the final state, what does the helper see, and what is in the audit log? Then: a 3 MB photo is 60% uploaded and the phone's battery dies; what happens on the next start, and can the job be submitted before the photo is complete?
4. In D1, the jobs arrive one by one from a stream and you must keep the best schedule so far: what breaks about "sort by end time", and what would you keep (an ordered map from end time to best profit, with floor lookups)? In C1, the data is 10^9 numbers on 100 machines: how do you find the median with a few rounds of communication (sample, count below / inside / above, narrow), and what is the cost in network round trips? In C2, the tree has duplicate values: what extra information makes the reconstruction unique? In G, one city holds 50,000 chargers inside a single grid cell: what is the symptom, what do you measure, and what do you change?
5. From your Enphase work (IoT Core, Lambda, SQS, event-driven notifications): pick ONE production problem you personally diagnosed (for example duplicate or missing notifications, an event that arrived late or twice, a device state that disagreed with the cloud, a queue that backed up) and tell it in five parts: the symptom, your first hypothesis, the evidence you used (a log line, a metric, a dump), the fix, and what you changed so it cannot happen silently again. Keep it to two minutes. Which number can you attach to the impact or the improvement, and which part were you personally responsible for? Use only what really happened.
